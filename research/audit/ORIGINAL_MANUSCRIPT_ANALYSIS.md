## 1. Abstract (Page 1)
- **What existing manuscript says:** Introduces CCSU as a response to rising atmospheric CO₂; lists major products (methanol, urea, formic acid, DMC, polycarbonates); mentions pharmaceutical/biotech applications; highlights energy penalties and catalyst constraints; asserts that CO₂ utilisation "supports global efforts to achieve net-zero emissions and increase possibilities of continued use of fossil fuels such as coal, petroleum and natural gas"; frames coverage for 2024–2026.
- **Scientific objective:** Summarise scope, technological routes, key challenges, and value proposition of CO₂ utilisation.
- **Main reactions/formulas:** None stated explicitly in abstract.
- **Existing evidence/citations:** None (standard for abstracts).
- **Scientific strengths:** Correctly identifies key industrial targets (methanol, urea, formic acid, polycarbonates) and recognizes core limitations (energy demand, catalyst constraints).
- **Flaws, ambiguities & unsupported claims:**
  1. *Critical Conceptual Error:* Claims CO₂ utilisation provides a rationale for the "continued use of fossil fuels such as coal, petroleum and natural gas". Thermodynamically, converting CO₂ from fossil combustion into fuels or chemicals requires significantly more energy than was released during combustion. Unless powered by zero-carbon primary energy, recycling fossil-derived CO₂ creates a net positive GHG footprint. Even with zero-carbon energy, utilisation cannot offset uncaptured fugitive emissions or justify prolonged fossil reliance.
  2. *Temporal Framing:* References "2024–2026" without presenting a verifiable systematic screening methodology.
- **Missing research (2020–2025):** Quantitative metrics on net climate mitigation potential, systemic energy penalties, and life-cycle boundary definitions.
- **New reactions/mechanisms:** Need clear demarcation between short-lived products (fuels), medium-lived (chemicals, polymers), and permanent storage (mineral carbonation).
- **Necessary corrections:** Remove the assertion that CCU enables continued unabated fossil-fuel extraction; frame CCU accurately as a circular carbon or carbon management tool for hard-to-abate sectors.
- **Status:** **REWRITE**

- **New reactions/mechanisms:** Clear thermodynamic landscape diagram contrasting downhill exergonic reactions ($\Delta G < 0$, e.g., mineral carbonation, urea synthesis) with uphill endergonic reactions ($\Delta G > 0$, e.g., RWGS, methanol, syngas, electrochemical reduction).

## 8. Methanol (Pages 9–10, including Figure 2)
- **What existing manuscript says:** Reaction: $\text{CO}_2 + 3\text{H}_2 \rightarrow \text{CH}_3\text{OH} + \text{H}_2\text{O}$ ($\Delta H_{298\text{K}} = -49.5\text{ kJ/mol}$). Catalysts: $\text{Cu/ZnO/Al}_2\text{O}_3$ at 200–300°C, 20–100 bar. Reaction pathway: $\text{CO}_2 \rightarrow \text{formate} \rightarrow \text{formaldehyde} \rightarrow \text{methoxy} \rightarrow \text{methanol}$. Notes that sustainability depends strictly on green hydrogen availability. Figure 2 shows industrial process flow.
- **Scientific objective:** Detail thermocatalytic methanol synthesis mechanisms, catalytic sites, and process flows.
- **Main reactions/formulas:**
  $$\text{CO}_2 + 3\text{H}_2 \rightleftharpoons \text{CH}_3\text{OH} + \text{H}_2\text{O} \quad (\Delta H_{298\text{K}} = -49.5\text{ kJ/mol})$$
  $$\text{CO}_2 + \text{H}_2 \rightleftharpoons \text{CO} + \text{H}_2\text{O} \quad (\Delta H_{298\text{K}} = +41.2\text{ kJ/mol, RWGS})$$
- **Existing evidence/citations:** Goeppert et al. (2014); Behrens et al. (2012); Kim et al. (2022).
- **Scientific strengths:** Correct stoichiometry and reaction enthalpy; cites seminal Behrens et al. (2012) Science paper on Cu/ZnO active sites; correctly identifies green $H_2$ reliance.
- **Flaws, ambiguities & unsupported claims:**
  1. *Competing RWGS Ignored in Math:* Neglects to include the endothermic reverse water-gas shift (RWGS) reaction and its thermodynamic equilibrium suppression of methanol yield at $T > 250^\circ\text{C}$.
  2. *Catalyst Innovations Missing:* Focuses entirely on conventional $\text{Cu/ZnO/Al}_2\text{O}_3$ (which suffers from water-induced sintering). Omits major recent catalysts: $\text{In}_2\text{O}_3$-based (e.g., $\text{In}_2\text{O}_3/\text{ZrO}_2$, Martin et al.), solid solution $\text{ZnO-ZrO}_2$ (Wang et al.), and intermetallics ($\text{Pd-In}, \text{Ni-Ga}$).
  3. *Stoichiometric Hydrogen Intensity:* Does not quantify that producing 1 tonne of $\text{CH}_3\text{OH}$ requires ~188–200 kg of $H_2$, consuming ~10–11 MWh of electrolysis electricity per tonne of methanol.
- **Missing research (2020–2025):** Industrial demonstration facilities (CRI George Olah Plant in Iceland, Shunli Plant in Anyang, China, Carbon Recycling International 110,000 t/yr); oxygen-vacancy mediated activation on $\text{In}_2\text{O}_3/\text{ZrO}_2$; dynamic water removal membrane reactors.
- **New reactions/mechanisms:** Formate pathway vs hydrocarboxyl pathway; $\text{In}_2\text{O}_3$ oxygen vacancy cycle ($\text{V}_\text{O} + \text{CO}_2 \rightarrow \text{CO}_2^* \rightarrow \text{HCOO}^* \rightarrow \text{H}_2\text{COO}^* \rightarrow \text{H}_2\text{CO}^* \rightarrow \text{H}_3\text{CO}^* \rightarrow \text{CH}_3\text{OH} + \text{O}^*$).
- **Necessary corrections:** Integrate competing RWGS thermodynamics; add 2020–2025 catalyst benchmarks ($\text{In}_2\text{O}_3$, $\text{ZnO-ZrO}_2$); provide quantitative hydrogen and carbon balances.
- **Status:** **UPDATE**

  $$\text{NH}_2\text{COONH}_4 \rightleftharpoons \text{CO(NH}_2)_2 + \text{H}_2\text{O} \quad (\Delta H_{298\text{K}} = +15.5\text{ kJ/mol})$$
- **Existing evidence/citations:** None cited in this section text.
- **Scientific strengths:** Correct two-step commercial mechanism (carbamate formation followed by endothermic dehydration); identifies it as largest global user (~160–180 Mt $\text{CO}_2/\text{yr}$).

  1. *Chemical Formula Typo:* Writes ammonium carbamate as $\text{NH}_4\text{NH}_2\text{COO}$ (incorrect chemical representation; should be $\text{NH}_2\text{COONH}_4$ or $[\text{NH}_4]^+[\text{NH}_2\text{COO}]^-$).

- **New reactions/mechanisms:**

  $$\text{CO}_2 + \text{NO}_3^- + 9\text{H}^+ + 8e^- \rightarrow \text{CO(NH}_2)_2 + 2\text{H}_2\text{O} + \text{OH}^-$$
- **Necessary corrections:** Fix formula typo for ammonium carbamate; explicitly analyze carbon permanence and life-cycle emissions; review emerging electrocatalytic C–N coupling literature (2020–2025).

## 10. Formic Acid (Pages 10–11)
- **What existing manuscript says:** Routes: (1) Thermal hydrogenation: $\text{CO}_2 + \text{H}_2 \rightarrow \text{HCOOH}$; (2) Electrochemical: $\text{CO}_2 + 2e^- + 2\text{H}^+ \rightarrow \text{HCOOH}$. Catalysts: Homogeneous (Ru, Ir), Heterogeneous (Sn, Bi, In, Pb electrodes). Mentions Bajracharya et al. (2017) DAC yield >75%. Discusses hydrogen storage and distributed production.
- **Scientific objective:** Compare thermocatalytic and electrochemical routes to formic acid/formate.
- **Main reactions/formulas:**
  $$\text{CO}_2 + \text{H}_2 \rightleftharpoons \text{HCOOH} \quad (\Delta G^\circ_{298\text{K}} = +32.9\text{ kJ/mol})$$
  $$\text{CO}_2 + 2\text{H}^+ + 2e^- \rightarrow \text{HCOOH} \quad (E^\circ = -0.25\text{ V vs. RHE})$$
  $$\text{CO}_2 + \text{H}_2\text{O} + 2e^- \rightarrow \text{HCOO}^- + \text{OH}^- \quad (\text{neutral/alkaline})$$
- **Existing evidence/citations:** De Luna et al. (2019); Bajracharya et al. (2017).
- **Scientific strengths:** Distinguishes thermal vs electrochemical pathways; lists representative p-block post-transition metal electrocatalysts (Sn, Bi, In, Pb).
- **Flaws, ambiguities & unsupported claims:**
  1. *Thermodynamic Equilibrium Unaddressed:* Thermal hydrogenation of CO₂ to pure formic acid is thermodynamically unfavored ($\Delta G^\circ > 0$); it requires basic additives (amines or inorganic bases) to drive equilibrium by forming formate salts or adducts, creating an energy-intensive product separation burden.
  2. *Formate Speciation Ignored:* Electrochemical reduction in neutral/alkaline media produces formate salts ($\text{HCOO}^-$), requiring downstream acidification (consuming acid and generating salt waste) or solid-electrolyte reactor configurations to yield pure liquid $\text{HCOOH}$.
  3. *Misleading Citation:* Bajracharya et al. (2017) is a bioelectrochemical study (*Faraday Discussions*), not a standard DAC-to-formic acid thermocatalytic process.
- **Missing research (2020–2025):** Solid-electrolyte flow cells (e.g., Wang group, Nature Energy 2020, producing 100 hr stable 20 wt% pure formic acid solution); bismuth-based catalysts ($\text{Bi}_2\text{O}_2\text{CO}_3$, Bi nanosheets) achieving FE >95% at >200 mA/cm²; techno-economics of liquid organic hydrogen carriers (LOHC).
- **New reactions/mechanisms:** Formate pathway over $p$-block metals: $* + \text{CO}_2 + e^- + \text{H}^+ \rightarrow \text{OCHO}^* \xrightarrow{e^- + \text{H}^+} \text{HCOOH} + *$.
- **Necessary corrections:** Differentiate molecular $\text{HCOOH}$ from $\text{HCOO}^-$; explain base-assisted thermal hydrogenation; highlight solid-electrolyte membrane flow cells.
- **Status:** **UPDATE**

  $$\text{CO}_2 + 2\text{Mg} \rightarrow 2\text{MgO} + \text{C} \quad (\Delta H_{298\text{K}} = -810\text{ kJ/mol})$$
- **Existing evidence/citations:** Bagotia et al. (2018a, 2018b, 2019); Sharma (2023); Kim et al. (2022); Yousuf et al. (2026).
